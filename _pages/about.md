I'm an economist working on how energy shocks and new infrastructure redraw trade and markets, with a particular eye on West Africa. I'm finishing an M.Sc. in Economics at **North Carolina State University** and begin a PhD in Natural Resource and Environmental Policy there in January 2027.

## What I'm working on

- **A refinery that rewires a region.** For decades Nigeria exported crude and imported fuel. I study how the Dangote Refinery is reshaping West African energy trade as the region's fuel flows reorganize around a new supplier.
- **Energy trade after the Russia shock.** How global energy networks found new partners and new exposures once Russian supply was cut off.
- **Data centers and the grid.** Where new digital infrastructure pushes electricity generation when transmission capacity is tight.
- **Land, crops, and options.** Whether agricultural options markets price the farmer's ability to switch between corn and soybeans.

My earlier work covers monetary policy across Sub-Saharan Africa, foreign aid and state stability in Afghanistan, and how geopolitical risk affects Nigerian banks. See [Publications](/publications/) for the full list.

## Before graduate school

I spent four years forecasting economies for a living. At **RiseVest Technologies** I led a team of five analysts building SVAR models to forecast U.S. inflation, trade balances, and GDP for investors. Before that, at **Gifted Analysts Research**, I built CPI and GDP models for Nigeria and other African economies. That work left me with a habit I bring to research: a model is only as good as the decisions it helps someone make.

I graduated top of a class of 148 with First Class Honors in Economics from the **University of Ilorin**, Nigeria.
